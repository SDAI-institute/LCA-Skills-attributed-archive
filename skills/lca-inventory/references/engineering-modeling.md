# Industrial foreground modeling

## Process definition

For each unit process record:

- function and quantitative reference;
- technology and operating mode;
- capacity, throughput, uptime/capacity factor;
- feed/product composition and state;
- conversion, yield, selectivity, recovery, losses;
- energy/utility demands and conditions;
- catalysts, solvents, consumables, water;
- direct emissions, wastewater, solids, waste treatment;
- control devices and efficiencies;
- co-products and quality;
- infrastructure, lifetime, maintenance, replacements;
- geography and data period.

## Data hierarchy

Prefer reconciled facility/supplier data. Use process simulation to complete or check physical balances, not to hide assumptions. Calibrate simulations against real operation or published benchmarks when possible.

## Annual-to-functional scaling

Ensure consistent production period, on-stream factor, capacity, yield, and reference flow. Allocate shared site utilities with a documented causal/physical/economic rule and test alternatives.

## Operating states

Include startup, shutdown, standby, cleaning, regeneration, flaring, off-spec production, maintenance, and seasonal changes when material. Average states using measured or scenario-specific frequency.

## Scale-up

For emerging systems, separate thermodynamic/stoichiometric quantities from scale-dependent equipment and utility assumptions. Use transparent scaling exponents, learning, heat integration, recovery, and capacity-factor scenarios; do not assume laboratory yield and commercial uptime simultaneously.
