# Claims and comparability controls

## Comparison prerequisites

A comparative public statement requires more than two numbers. Confirm alignment of:

- intended use and audience;
- product function, performance, quality, lifetime, and reference flow;
- PCR/PEFCR/program and validity period;
- modules/system boundary and scenarios;
- geography, technology, temporal representativeness;
- electricity and energy accounting;
- allocation, recycling, co-product, biogenic carbon, and end-of-life rules;
- database and LCIA method versions;
- data quality and verification status;
- uncertainty and material exclusions.

## Claim language

Use language matched to evidence:

- “lower modeled impact under the stated assumptions” rather than “greener”;
- “result is not statistically distinguishable” when uncertainty overlaps under the selected test;
- “screening estimate” for proxy-heavy studies;
- “verified EPD” only after the applicable program verification and publication steps;
- “carbon footprint” only for the defined climate indicator and boundary, not total sustainability.

## Prohibited shortcuts

- comparing declared units that deliver different service;
- comparing EPD values from incompatible PCRs or modules without reconciliation;
- subtracting offsets from the product inventory unless the governing rule explicitly permits it;
- presenting a single weighted score as objective superiority;
- hiding unfavorable life-cycle stages in a partial footprint;
- implying precision beyond data and model uncertainty.

Keep a claim-evidence matrix with exact result, scope, evidence class, uncertainty, approved wording, reviewer, and release channel.
