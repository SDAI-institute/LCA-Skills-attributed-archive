# Agriculture, forestry, food, and bio-based systems

## Functional unit

Mass alone may not represent function. Consider edible product, dry matter, protein, energy/nutrient delivery, quality, shelf life, cooking loss, or land occupation, while avoiding nutrition claims unsupported by the study.

## Inventory

Seeds/stock, fertilizer and lime, pesticides, irrigation, field energy, machinery, soil emissions, enteric/manure emissions, land occupation and land-use change, residue management, yield, rotations, co-products, storage, processing, cold chain, packaging, retail, cooking, food loss/waste, and disposal.

## Direct emissions

Use transparent models for N2O, NH3, NOx, nitrate/phosphate leaching/runoff, CH4, soil carbon, enteric fermentation, manure, rice, and residue burning. Match climate, soil, practice, and time horizon. Separate biogenic carbon flows and fossil emissions.

## Allocation and land

Co-products, manure, residues, and energy recovery require explicit functionality and market treatment. Do not credit residues that are not surplus. Distinguish direct and indirect land-use change, and avoid hiding land occupation/biodiversity impacts behind climate-only results.

## Checks

- nutrient N/P/K balance and fertilizer nutrient basis;
- wet/dry mass and edible yield consistency;
- crop yield, animal growth, feed conversion, mortality, and product output;
- carbon stock change permanence and baseline;
- food loss expands all upstream production appropriately;
- organic, local, bio-based, or regenerative labels are not impact conclusions without measured system evidence.
