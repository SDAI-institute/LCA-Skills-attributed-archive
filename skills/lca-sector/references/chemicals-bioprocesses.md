# Chemicals, refining, polymers, and bioprocesses

## Functional unit

Define chemical identity, concentration, purity, phase, pressure/temperature, packaging, and delivery location. For performance chemicals or materials, use the delivered function where formulations differ.

## Process model

Include reaction stoichiometry, conversion, selectivity, yield, recycle/purge, separations, solvents, catalysts, adsorbents, inert gases, steam levels, cooling/refrigeration, vacuum, compression, cleaning, wastewater, off-gas control, utilities, and by-products. Separate plant-average and product-specific burdens transparently.

For bioprocesses include inoculum/seed train, media nutrients, sterilization, aeration/agitation, oxygen transfer, pH control, antifoam, fermentation yield/productivity/titer, cell removal, concentration, purification, solvent recovery, water-for-injection quality where applicable, and biological waste treatment.

## Direct emissions

Calculate reaction and combustion emissions from carbon/element balances when possible. Include fugitive VOCs, methane, N2O, acid gases, flaring, venting, wastewater COD/BOD/nitrogen, solvent losses, refrigerants, and catalyst disposal. Do not infer zero emissions from missing permit data.

## Multifunctionality

Refineries and biorefineries have coupled products. Test subdivision, physical relationships, economic allocation, and system expansion against the goal. Prices need region/year/currency and sensitivity.

## Checks

- elemental closure for C/H/O/N/S/halogens where material;
- theoretical and reported yield consistency;
- solvent recovery cannot exceed input and recovered quality must be usable;
- utility steam/heat temperatures are compatible;
- wastewater loads follow unrecovered material;
- lab-scale dilution and chromatography assumptions are not blindly scaled linearly;
- bio-based does not automatically mean carbon neutral or low impact.
