# Water, land, biodiversity, and toxicity

## Water

Inventory withdrawal and consumption separately by source and location. Return flows, quality change, evaporation, incorporation, and basin scarcity matter. A litre used in one basin is not equivalent to another.

## Land

Distinguish occupation and transformation, prior/next land use, duration, location, soil carbon, and land-use change. Biodiversity indicators are method- and context-sensitive; disclose what taxa/ecosystems and mechanisms are represented or omitted.

## Toxicity

Characterization factors represent comparative potential, not site-specific exposure or regulatory risk. Check chemical identity, speciation, compartment, subcompartment, emission location, indoor/outdoor pathways, and factor status. Results may span orders of magnitude uncertainty.

## Particulates and air pollution

Ensure emitted substance and formation pathway mapping are correct. Stack height, location, population exposure, and precursor fate may be simplified in generic methods.

## Resource indicators

Mineral/fossil/resource scarcity indicators encode different concepts—depletion, surplus cost, energy content, or dissipation. State indicator meaning before making “resource depletion” claims.
