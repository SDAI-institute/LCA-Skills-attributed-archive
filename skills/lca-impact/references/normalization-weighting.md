# Normalization, weighting, and single scores

## Normalization

Divides characterized results by a reference such as regional/global per-capita or total impacts. Record reference population, year, geography, method, and whether factors match characterization. Normalized magnitude does not by itself prove severity or priority.

## Weighting

Applies value-based preferences across impact categories. Record stakeholder/value basis, factor set, date, and sensitivity. Weighting can support interpretation but cannot erase trade-offs or scientific uncertainty.

## Single scores

Report characterized results alongside normalized/weighted results. Do not use a single score for public comparative assertions where applicable rules prohibit or constrain weighting. Test alternative weighting sets and identify categories driving the score.

## Decision use

Keep environmental results distinct from cost, technical performance, social, or risk criteria unless a transparent multi-criteria decision method is defined. Do not imply that one aggregated score is an objective fact.
