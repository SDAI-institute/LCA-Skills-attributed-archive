# Expert reasoning protocol

## 1. Diagnose before calculating

Translate the request into a decision, comparison, or descriptive question. Identify which unknowns can materially affect the decision. Ask only blocking questions; handle non-blocking gaps with explicit scenarios.

## 2. Maintain an assumption ledger

Every material assumption needs:

- statement and rationale;
- affected processes/results;
- evidence/source class;
- baseline and plausible alternatives;
- uncertainty or sensitivity treatment;
- owner and validation status.

Do not let assumptions remain only in prose or chat.

## 3. Use an evidence hierarchy

Prefer, in order appropriate to the question:

1. applicable legal/program requirements and current standards;
2. measured supplier/facility data with QA and basis;
3. official technical datasets/models and primary government data;
4. peer-reviewed primary studies with transparent inventory;
5. industry primary reports with clear methods;
6. high-quality reviews and databases;
7. analogues/proxies;
8. expert judgment.

Authority does not guarantee representativeness; evaluate both.

## 4. Check dimensions and conservation

Before trusting a result:

- trace the reference flow through all scaling;
- write expected units for each formula;
- convert wet/dry, concentration, density, energy, standard volume, and currency-year bases explicitly;
- close mass, elemental carbon/nitrogen, energy, or charge balances where relevant;
- compare implied intensity with physical limits.

## 5. Triangulate

Use at least one independent lens for material results:

- hand calculation;
- stoichiometric minimum/maximum;
- industry energy/material intensity;
- alternate database or published range;
- process simulation;
- facility bill/production reconciliation;
- carbon or mass balance.

Investigate discrepancies before choosing the preferred answer.

## 6. Separate uncertainty types

Do not collapse these into one generic error bar:

- measurement/data uncertainty;
- variability across facilities/regions/time;
- parameter uncertainty;
- model structure and allocation choices;
- scenario/counterfactual uncertainty;
- characterization-model uncertainty.

Monte Carlo on parameter distributions does not capture all model uncertainty.

## 7. Control precision

Report digits supported by the data and purpose. A result calculated to six decimals from proxy inputs is not six-decimal knowledge. Round after aggregation, preserve machine precision in internal artifacts, and state the rounding rule.

## 8. Treat anomalies as audit triggers

Investigate:

- negative net impacts;
- credits larger than gross burdens;
- zero contribution from expected hotspots;
- orders-of-magnitude changes after a minor edit;
- rankings dependent on one unverified proxy;
- 100% mapping despite unusual flows;
- improbable mass/energy intensity;
- results that exactly mirror an input factor without the expected supply chain.

## 9. Know when to escalate

Require specialist/reviewer input for high-stakes public claims, formal verification, novel toxicology, land-use change, biodiversity, social/human-rights findings, safety-critical process data, legal/regulatory interpretations, or unresolved model choices that can reverse conclusions.
