# Social life cycle assessment

Use the current applicable social-LCA standard/guidance. Social risk screening, organizational due diligence, and measured site performance are different evidence levels.

## Design

Define decision, stakeholder groups, impact pathways/topics, activity variable, geography, time period, unit of analysis, value-chain tiers, data sources, and intended interpretation. Typical stakeholder groups include workers, local communities, value-chain actors, consumers, children, society, and rights holders.

## Evidence levels

- Country/sector risk databases support hotspot screening, not allegations about a specific supplier.
- Audits and certifications provide bounded evidence and can miss hidden or temporary labor conditions.
- Worker/community engagement and grievance data require ethical protocols and context.
- Social indicators may be qualitative, ordinal, quantitative, or mixed; do not force false cardinal precision.

## Safeguards

Protect personal and sensitive data, minimize harm, obtain appropriate consent, avoid retaliatory exposure, document uncertainty and bias, and involve human-rights/social-science expertise. Provide a process for correction and stakeholder response.

## Integration

Keep social findings separate from environmental LCIA unless an explicit, transparent multi-criteria framework is selected. Weighting is normative. Report hotspots, evidence strength, affected groups, improvement options, and unresolved risks rather than a single “social sustainability” score.
