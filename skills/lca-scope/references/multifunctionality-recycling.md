# Multifunctionality and recycling protocol

## Decision hierarchy

1. Avoid allocation by subdividing the process where physically/data feasible.
2. Expand the system or redefine function when it matches the decision and applicable rules.
3. Partition using an underlying physical/causal relationship.
4. Use another relationship such as economic value when justified and allowed.

Record the governing program/standard rule; do not choose the method that produces the preferred result.

## Partitioning checks

- Identify all valuable products and waste-treatment functions.
- Define allocation property, basis year, prices/physical properties, and normalization.
- Test price volatility or physical-key uncertainty.
- Ensure inputs, direct emissions, and upstream burdens are partitioned consistently.
- Distinguish process subdivision from arbitrary allocation.

## Substitution/system expansion checks

- Specify displaced function/product, substitution ratio, quality correction, geography, technology, time, and market feasibility.
- Avoid assigning the same avoided product to multiple systems.
- Show gross burdens and credits separately.
- Test alternate displaced suppliers.

## Recycling frameworks

Common approaches include recycled-content/cut-off, end-of-life/avoided-burden, allocation at point of substitution, 50/50 or shared-burden formulas, and program-specific circular footprint formulas. They encode different responsibility principles and should not be mixed casually.

Record:

- recycled content;
- collection/sorting/reprocessing yields;
- quality loss/downcycling;
- end-of-waste point;
- disposal and treatment burdens;
- secondary-material substitution;
- number of cycles if modeled;
- ownership of recycling benefit.
