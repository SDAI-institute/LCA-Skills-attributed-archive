# Functional unit and reference flow

## Functional unit construction

Express:

`quantity of function + performance/quality + duration + operating conditions + geography/time where material`

Examples of structure, not default answers:

- packaging: delivery/protection of a specified product quantity at damage and shelf-life performance;
- building product: provision of a defined thermal/structural/acoustic function over reference service life;
- transport: passenger-kilometre or tonne-kilometre with occupancy/payload, vehicle class, route, and service conditions;
- energy: delivered useful energy at quality, reliability, location, and time profile;
- chemical: quantity meeting purity/composition and performance specification;
- wastewater: treatment of a specified volume/load from influent quality to effluent requirement;
- battery: delivered lifetime energy/throughput at power, efficiency, depth-of-discharge, degradation, and service conditions;
- food: nutritional/service function when mass alone distorts comparison.

## Reference flow

The reference flow is the quantity of product system output required to fulfill the functional unit. It includes replacements, losses, efficiency, occupancy, yield, maintenance, or service-life scaling.

## Challenge tests

- Can two alternatives satisfy the same unit while delivering materially different service?
- Does lifetime or degradation change required quantity?
- Are quality/purity/performance thresholds explicit?
- Is the use pattern realistic and symmetric?
- Does the reference flow reconcile with product-system quantitative references?

## Declared unit

When function cannot be established—common in intermediate products or EPD modules—use a declared unit and state that comparisons require additional functional equivalence and downstream context.
