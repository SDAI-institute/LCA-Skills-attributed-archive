# System boundary and completeness

## Boundary dimensions

Document:

- life-cycle stages: raw materials, manufacture, distribution, use, maintenance, replacements, end of life;
- foreground/background distinction;
- capital goods/infrastructure;
- personnel/administration where relevant;
- direct land transformation/occupation;
- upstream energy and material supply;
- emissions controls, wastewater, waste management;
- co-products, recovered materials/energy, and avoided functions;
- geographical and temporal boundary;
- technological state and operating modes.

## Boundary types

Use descriptive labels such as cradle-to-gate, gate-to-gate, cradle-to-grave, well-to-pump, well-to-wheels, tank-to-wheels, or module-based EPD boundaries, but also list included/excluded processes. Labels alone are insufficient.

## Cutoff

Define criteria by mass, energy, environmental relevance, data availability, or program rule. Avoid a blanket percentage that could exclude highly impactful low-mass flows. Track cumulative exclusions and perform a screening estimate for potentially material omitted flows.

## Symmetry rule

Comparative systems must treat equivalent stages, infrastructure, burdens, credits, quality losses, and end-of-life functions symmetrically. Asymmetric data quality is itself a sensitivity case.

## Boundary diagram

Show processes, included/excluded stages, elementary flows, technosphere links, co-products, recycling loops, credits, and handoff points to external models.
