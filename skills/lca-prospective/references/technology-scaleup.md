# Emerging-technology scale-up

Scale-up must preserve physical plausibility and distinguish measured performance from projected performance.

## Technology readiness record

Record: current scale, target scale, unit operation, batch/continuous mode, annual capacity, operating hours, yield, selectivity, conversion, recovery, purity, recycle, utility demand, lifetime, degradation, replacement, control requirements, and evidence class.

## Scale-up methods

Use the least speculative method adequate for the decision:

1. **Engineering design** — stoichiometry, balances, equipment sizing, heat integration, separations, utility systems, and emissions control.
2. **Analogous commercial process** — adjust a transparent reference process for capacity, chemistry, material, and performance differences.
3. **Scaling relation** — use `Q_new/Q_ref` exponents only for the relevant cost/material/energy item and justify the operating regime.
4. **Learning or experience curve** — define cumulative production, learning rate, floor, and whether improvement affects CAPEX, energy, yield, or material intensity.
5. **Scenario envelope** — represent unresolved designs as coherent conservative, central, and advanced cases.

## Mandatory checks

- Stoichiometric lower bounds for feedstock and reagent use.
- Thermodynamic or separation-energy plausibility.
- Yield/selectivity consistency across linked steps.
- Capacity-factor consistency with annual output.
- Equipment/material lifetime and replacement count.
- Laboratory solvent, carrier gas, cleaning, and excess-reagent assumptions after scale-up.
- Commercial purity/specification and downstream conditioning.
- Wastewater, off-gas, purge, catalyst, adsorbent, and consumable treatment.
- Burden-shifting from heat integration or recycling loops.
- Supply constraints for critical minerals, biomass, CO2, hydrogen, or electricity.

## Fair comparison

Compare the emerging and incumbent systems in the same geography, year, service quality, utilization, policy context, and background scenario. Apply plausible learning to both where appropriate. Report break-even values rather than only an optimistic point estimate.
