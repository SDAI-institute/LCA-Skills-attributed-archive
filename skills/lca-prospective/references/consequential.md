# Consequential LCA decision logic

Consequential LCA estimates changes caused by a decision. Every credited or induced change needs a causal mechanism.

## Causal chain

For each material market effect, document:

1. decision and scale;
2. demand or supply change;
3. affected market and time horizon;
4. market trend and constraints;
5. marginal supplier/technology or displaced function;
6. rebound, leakage, co-production, and capacity effects;
7. evidence and uncertainty;
8. sign and magnitude check.

## Marginal supplier screening

A supplier is not marginal merely because it has the highest impact. Test whether it can respond to the modeled change given geography, trade, capacity, regulation, lead time, technology lock-in, seasonality, and market trend. Use multiple plausible marginals when evidence is weak.

## Multifunctionality

- Prefer subdivision where physical processes can be separated.
- System expansion/substitution must represent an actually displaced product or service.
- Match quality, location, timing, and quantity of substituted function.
- Constrain substitution by market size and technical limits.
- Avoid double claiming the same avoided burden across supplier and user systems.

## Indirect effects

Include land-use change, market-mediated production shifts, rebound, induced infrastructure, or policy interactions only with a defensible model and transparent uncertainty. Do not use a qualitative possibility as a precise numeric adjustment.

## Interpretation

Report gross changes and credits separately, identify parameters that control the sign, and state the causal claim narrowly. A conclusion that changes under plausible marginal-supplier choices is scenario-dependent, not robust.
